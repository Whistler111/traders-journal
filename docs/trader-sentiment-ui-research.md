# Trader sentiment and journal usability

Researched 19 October 2026. Findings below use original provider, regulator and UX-research sources. Product recommendations are design inferences, not evidence that a particular field will improve trading returns. No trader interviews or live sentiment feed were collected.

## Two different meanings of sentiment

**Market sentiment** describes a defined population or market measure. **Personal state** records the trader's own feelings and expectations. Keep them separate in the UI and analysis: feeling confident does not establish bullish market positioning, and a bullish survey does not establish a good entry.

| Source                                                                                                         | What it measures                                                                                   | Fit for this journal                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [AAII survey](https://www.aaii.com/sentiment-survey)                                                           | Weekly individual-investor bullish/neutral/bearish views on US stocks over six months              | Broader stock-market context; unsuitable as an intraday XAU/USD sentiment meter.                                                                        |
| [CFTC Commitments of Traders](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm)               | Reported futures positioning by participant category; generally Tuesday positions published Friday | Context for a specifically mapped futures contract. Show observation date and release date; do not relabel it live spot-gold or universal FX sentiment. |
| [IG client sentiment](https://www.ig.com/uk/trading-strategies/_how-to-trade-using-ig-client-sentiment-240912) | Long/short positioning among IG retail clients                                                     | A broker-specific sample, not the entire market. Preserve the provider's counting method and instrument coverage.                                       |
| [TradingView Technical Ratings](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000614331-technical-ratings/)  | Composite scores from technical indicators                                                         | Technical context, not a survey of trader emotions. Keep the exact symbol/feed and timeframe visible.                                                   |

Do not build a single unlabeled “market confidence” percentage from these incompatible sources. Before integrating a feed, verify its API access, permitted reuse, coverage, timestamps and cost. Public display pages alone do not establish an available free API.

## What the behavioral evidence suggests

The [FCA's 2024 experiment](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-keeps-trading-apps-under-review-over-gaming-concerns), involving more than 9,000 consumers, found that some digital engagement practices can increase trading frequency and risk taking. This supports a restrained journal design: reward completing a thoughtful review rather than opening more trades. It does not establish the effect of this app's particular animation.

[CFA Institute's behavioral-finance overview](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/articles/behavioral-finance-careers) discusses how psychological factors and biases such as overconfidence and herd behavior affect decisions. Translate this into optional, factual reflection prompts rather than diagnoses, predictive emotion scores or labels that shame a loss.

| P1       | A long narrative is hard to write on a phone                      | Keep one short required entry reason; offer optional prompts: “What would invalidate this idea?” and “What will I repeat or change?”                                                                | Abandonment and usefulness of reviews; do not require longer notes as a success metric.             |
| P1       | Personal outlook and actual position direction are different      | Optional “My market view: bullish / bearish / neutral / unsure” plus timeframe, alongside a separately labeled Buy/Long or Sell/Short direction.                                                    | Whether traders understand the distinction in usability sessions.                                   |
| P1       | One emotion label mixes state, confidence and urgency             | Optional check-in: feeling, confidence low/medium/high, and “Did I feel rushed?” Keep it short and skippable.                                                                                       | Can a trader complete it quickly without interpreting a score?                                      |
| P1       | Existing playbooks can reduce repeated work                       | Place playbook selection earlier and let a trader reuse context, while never guessing prices, original risk or realized results.                                                                    | Fewer repeated edits; no copied financial values without confirmation.                              |
| P1       | Traders need closure beyond the P&L result                        | Add a small “What I followed / one adjustment” review and a neutral “Done for today” action. Preserve manual sign-out.                                                                              | Review completion and perceived clarity, not trading frequency.                                     |
| P2       | Market sentiment needs provenance                                 | An optional collapsed context panel: source, exact instrument, timeframe, observation/publication timestamps and stale/unavailable state. Save only a user-selected trade-time snapshot, not ticks. | Accurate labeling, failed-feed behavior and no unsolicited trading signals.                         |
| P2       | Emotion comparisons can look more conclusive than the data allows | Show closed-trade count with any mood/process grouping, keep “Not recorded” separate, and label comparisons exploratory.                                                                            | Small-sample comprehension; never claim an emotion causes profit.                                   |

[NN/G's form research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/required-fields/) recommends clearly marking required fields. Required original-risk inputs should not be presented as optional-looking advanced settings. Keep persistent labels, 16 px mobile input text, at least 44 px tap targets, inline explanations and entered values after a failed save.

## Recommended delivery sequence

1. Move required risk fields into the main flow, clarify open/closed paths, and label every optional field. These have higher priority than adding a sentiment dashboard.
2. Introduce optional personal-state metadata and short reflection prompts. Preserve version-1 backups with additive fields and keep current financial fields/calculations unchanged.
3. Validate with a small set of actual traders using realistic open, breakeven, losing, partial-close and missing-data scenarios. Measure the task, not engagement for its own sake. Use consented, minimal research records; do not upload sensitive journal text as analytics.
4. Consider external sentiment only after choosing a named source and permitted API. No forecast, profitability guarantee or trade recommendation is implied by the panel.

This research does not implement the proposed fields or change saved records. The present delivery adds a visible dashboard sign-out control and public SEO/brand wiring only.
